Plotlines

Privacy Policy

Effective October 24, 2026 · Applies to the Plotlines Android app and the Plotlines web app

The short version

  • Everything you write in Plotlines (goals, steps, habits, journal moments, lists, threads, photos, conversations with the assistant, canvases and boards) is stored on your own device.
  • Plotlines has no servers of its own and we never receive, see or store your data.
  • AI runs on your device by default. Your words go to an AI service only if you choose one yourself (your own AI server, or an AI provider with your own key) (section 5).
  • If you turn on sync, your data is copied to a private, hidden folder in your own Google Drive. Only Plotlines on your devices can read it, and only you can delete it.
  • Things you choose to connect or use go straight from your device to that service, never through us: Google Calendar, Gmail and Drive for the agent, Notion, free public services for weather, places and quick facts, and end-to-end encrypted sharing (section 6).
  • No accounts with us, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, no cookies, and we never sell or share your data.
  • You can export or erase everything at any time.

1. Who we are

Plotlines is a personal planning app for life goals, daily goals, habits, routines, lists and journaling, with an assistant called Plot. It is developed independently by Munazzar (“we”, “us”). This policy explains what information Plotlines handles, where it is kept, who can access it and how you control it.

Because Plotlines works without a server of ours, we are not able to access your information. In data-protection terms, you are in control of your own data at all times.

2. What Plotlines handles

Content you create

  • Goals and sub-goals: titles, reasons (“why it matters”), life area, horizon, priority, dates, links between goals, cover images, pinned status and check-in times.
  • Steps: titles, notes, due dates and times, reminders, repeat settings, completion history and focus-timer minutes.
  • Daily goals, reminders, timers and check-ins, with their dates, times and repeats.
  • Habits and routines: names, schedules, amounts, reminders, routine steps, daily check-offs, rest days, pauses, notes, and for habits you are breaking: start time, slips (time, trigger and optional note), urges resisted, daily counts and any cost or time figures you enter.
  • Journal moments: titles, text, handwriting and drawings, photos, plus automatic entries such as completed steps and milestones. Weekly reviews you write.
  • Threads (notes over time) and lists, with their items, due dates and check-offs.
  • Your own activity trackers (for example yoga minutes or pages read) and the amounts you log each day.
  • Activity read on your Android phone, only for the kinds you allow: steps (including steps by hour), distance, calories, sleep and workouts from Health Connect, your phone’s step counter, minutes spent in each app (screen time), and since 1.54 which apps you used in each hour of the day and how many times you unlocked the phone, and time spent at places you saved. It is kept for up to 180 days.
  • What you do with the AI: conversations with the assistant and in Live talk, Ask threads, AI canvases (pages the AI builds for you), Workbench boards, agent tasks and their results, insights, and “Picture of me”, a description of you that the AI writes from your data when you ask for it.

Settings and preferences

Your first name (optional), the assistant’s name, theme and display choices, layout choices, reminder and voice preferences, saved places, and a PIN if you set one (stored only as a one-way hash).

Keys and sign-ins you enter

The email address of a Google account you connect, short-lived Google access tokens, a Notion integration token if you add one, and the key for your own AI server if you enter one. Google tokens stay on the device where you signed in. The Notion token and the AI server key stay on your device and, only if you turn on sync, are also kept in your own Drive’s hidden app folder so your other devices can use them (see section 3).

What Plotlines does not collect

Plotlines does not send us anything. It does not collect contacts, device identifiers, advertising IDs, usage analytics or crash reports. It does not record or keep audio.

4. Google sign-in and Drive sync

Sync is optional and off until you turn it on. When you connect Google Drive:

  • Sign-in. On Android, you pick a Google account on your device, or sign in through your browser. On the web, you sign in on Google’s own page. We never see your Google password. Plotlines only accepts a sign-in that it started itself, so a link made by someone else can’t connect your device to their account.
  • Permissions requested. For sync, Plotlines asks for drive.appdata, which lets it create and read its own hidden files in your Drive and nothing else: it can’t see, open or change your other Drive files. Other Google permissions are asked for only when you turn on the feature that needs them: your email address (userinfo.email) when you allow sharing invites, and Calendar, Gmail (read only) or Drive file names for the agent (section 6).
  • Your email address. Plotlines reads the email address of the connected account, shows it in Settings, checks it before syncing so one account’s data never merges into another’s by accident, and uses it to confirm it’s you if you reset a forgotten PIN.
  • How data travels. Your device talks directly to Google’s servers over encrypted HTTPS connections. Nothing passes through any server of ours, because we don’t run one.
  • When it syncs. When you make changes, when you open the app, every few minutes while it is open, and when you pull down to refresh. If two devices changed the same item, the most recent change wins.
  • Turning it off. Settings → Sync → Turn off sync. That device stops syncing. Your Drive copy stays until you delete it (see section 12).

Plotlines’ use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Data from Google is used only to provide the features you turned on, for you. It is never used for advertising, never sold, never transferred to others, never used to train AI models, and never read by a person.

5. AI and voice

Plot (the assistant and Live talk), Ask, Insights, Plan with AI, the agent, AI canvas, Picture of me, suggestions and “Fill in today” all use the AI you pick in Settings › AI & voice:

  • On-device AI (the default). An open-source model is downloaded once (from Hugging Face, with the code that runs it from jsDelivr) and then runs on your phone or in your browser. Your questions and data stay on your device.
  • Your own AI server, only if you enter one (for example Ollama or LM Studio on your computer, or a service such as OpenRouter). Your question and the parts of your data needed to answer it are sent to that address and handled under its terms. We never see them.
  • AI providers with your own key, only in Workbench and only if you pick one (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini, Groq, Mistral, DeepSeek, xAI or OpenRouter). The board or prompt you run goes straight from your device to that provider, under its terms.
  • Copy and paste. Plan with AI and Workbench can build a prompt that you copy into an assistant of your choice (for example Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini). Read the prompt before you paste it, and leave out anything you don’t want to share.

What the AI is given: what the feature needs, such as your question and the notes that match it, a summary of recent habits, goals, activity and journal entries for insights, or your data for a canvas that you ask to use it. Journal entries you mark private are left out unless you include them in Settings.

AI canvas. Pages the AI builds run in a locked-down frame that can’t reach the rest of the app. A page only receives your data if its code asks for it and “Use my data” is on, and such a page is kept offline: it can load code libraries from jsDelivr and fonts from Google Fonts, and nothing else, so it can’t send your data anywhere.

Voice. Talking to Plot and “Hey Plot” use speech recognition on your device: Android’s recognizer, or your browser’s on the web. Depending on your phone and browser, these may use their maker’s online speech service (for example Google’s), under that maker’s policy. Spoken replies use your device’s voices or Plotlines’ natural voices, which run on the device after a one-time download from jsDelivr and Hugging Face. Some of your device’s own voices may be online voices from its maker. Plotlines does not record or keep audio.

Photo to text and handwriting are read on your device. The models are downloaded once from Plotlines’ own website (GitHub Pages) and jsDelivr.

6. The agent, the web and sharing

  • Agent connections (Settings › Agent & connections), each off until you turn it on. Google Calendar: reads your events and adds an event only when you tap to add it. Gmail: searches and reads messages when a task needs them, read only; it never sends or deletes mail. Google Drive: finds files by name and date, without opening them. Notion: reads pages you shared with your own integration, using a token you paste in; on the web this goes through a proxy address you set up yourself. What the agent reads is used for your answer, and results are kept in the agent’s inbox on that device only. Text inside emails and pages is treated as information, never as instructions to the agent.
  • The web (on by default, can be turned off in Agent & connections). For live information, such as the weather, places and opening hours, or a quick fact, the AI may look things up with free public services: Open-Meteo (weather and place names), OpenStreetMap Nominatim (places and opening hours), DuckDuckGo and Wikipedia (quick facts), and for news: Google News and Google Trends (in the Android app, or through a proxy you set), GDELT, Wikipedia’s Current events and Hacker News, plus any page or RSS feed a task names. They receive the search words and, for “near me”, a position rounded to about 1 km. If your position isn’t available, a rough location from your IP address is looked up once with GeoJS. No account is used, and your plan, emails and journal are not sent.
  • Places. When you add a place for an automatic check-off, the address you type and a rounded position (about 1 km) go to Photon (photon.komoot.io) to find it. “Use my location” and moving the pin send a position rounded to about 10 m once, to look up the address. The small map loads its pictures from OpenStreetMap’s tile server, which sees which area is shown.
  • Doing it together. When you share one goal, habit, step or list with people you invite, an end-to-end encrypted copy of just that item (its title, steps and mode, and each person’s name, check-ins and reactions) is kept in Plotlines’ sharing space on Google Firebase so everyone in it stays in step. Only the people in it can read it. When the owner stops sharing, the shared copy is deleted; anyone who leaves has their progress removed from it. So that people can invite you, Plotlines registers your email address and your public key (never the private one) in the sharing space once you allow invites while sync is on. You can turn this off in Settings › Sharing.
  • Share links for Workbench boards and lists. The copy in Firebase is encrypted with a key that exists only in the link itself, so Firebase and Google see only scrambled data; anyone you give the link to can open it. Boards and lists that come from a link are checked when they arrive, so they can only contain plain text, numbers and pictures.
  • App updates (Android only). Plotlines checks GitHub at most every 6 hours for a newer version. GitHub sees your IP address, nothing else.

7. Notifications, widgets and files

  • Reminders and notifications are scheduled on your device using Android’s alarm service, or by your browser while the web app is open. No push server is involved. Notification text can appear on your lock screen, depending on your device’s notification settings.
  • Home-screen widgets show titles, progress and habit status on your home screen. Anyone who can see your home screen can see them.
  • Sending to Plotlines. Text you share to Plotlines from another app, or select and send with “Plotlines”, goes to the assistant on your device.
  • Sending from Plotlines opens your device’s share sheet. Where it goes is up to you. Calendar export creates a standard calendar file that your calendar app then handles.
  • Links to AI assistants, Google sign-in and websites open in your browser or the relevant app.

8. Android permissions

Each one is asked for only when you use the feature that needs it, and you can turn any of them off in Android settings.

  • Internet: for sync, sharing, the agent’s connections and web lookups, address search, update checks, downloading AI models and voices, and your own AI server if you set one.
  • Notifications, exact alarms, full-screen alerts, run at startup, vibrate and wake lock: to deliver the reminders you set on time, restore them after a restart, and give feedback when you check something off.
  • Physical activity (activity recognition) and Health Connect read access to steps, distance, calories, sleep and exercise: for the Activity page and automatic habit check-offs. Plotlines never writes to Health Connect.
  • Usage access: to show screen time: minutes in each app, which apps you used in each hour, and unlocks. Only which app was open and when is read, never what you do inside it. It’s asked for only after Plotlines explains this, stays on your phone and in your own Drive if sync is on, and can be turned off in Android’s settings.
  • Location, including in the background: only if you save a place for an automatic check-off, used on the phone to notice when you are there; and while you use “near me” in the agent.
  • Microphone and a foreground service: for talking to Plot and “Hey Plot”, only while listening is on.
  • Biometrics: to unlock Plotlines with your fingerprint or face if you turn on App lock.
  • Display over other apps and ignore battery optimisation: so the assistant and reminders can appear when you call them, and are not stopped by battery saving.

Plotlines does not request camera, storage or contacts permissions. When you add a photo, Android’s own camera or file picker opens, and Plotlines receives only the image you choose.

9. Every outside service, in one list

Plotlines only contacts these, and only for the reason given. Each one sees your IP address, as any website does.

  • Google: sign-in, Drive sync, and Calendar, Gmail and Drive for the agent, each only if you turn it on. Google’s Privacy Policy applies to data in your Google account.
  • Google Firebase: the encrypted sharing space, sharing sign-in and share links (section 6).
  • GitHub: GitHub Pages hosts the web app and the handwriting and photo-to-text models; the Android app checks GitHub for updates. See GitHub’s Privacy Statement. The Android app loads its own pages from inside the app.
  • Hugging Face, jsDelivr, esm.sh and unpkg: downloads of on-device AI models, voices and the open-source code that runs them, and code libraries for AI canvases.
  • Open-Meteo, OpenStreetMap (Nominatim and map tiles), Photon, DuckDuckGo, Wikipedia and GeoJS: weather, places, addresses and quick facts (section 6).
  • Google News, Google Trends, GDELT and Hacker News (Algolia): news and trending topics for agent tasks; they receive the search words only (section 6).
  • Notion: only if you add your Notion token.
  • AI servers and AI providers you choose (section 5), and apps you share to.

Plotlines includes no third-party SDKs, analytics libraries, advertising networks or crash reporters. The app’s fonts are bundled and not loaded from outside services.

10. What we never do

  • Run servers or databases that hold your data.
  • Sell, rent or share your personal information.
  • Show ads or use your data for advertising.
  • Track you across apps or websites, or use cookies.
  • Collect analytics, usage statistics or crash reports.
  • Read your content, or train AI models on it.

11. Security

  • Your data lives inside Plotlines’ private storage on your device, which other apps cannot read. Modern Android devices encrypt this storage when the device is locked with a screen lock. We recommend using one.
  • All network traffic uses encrypted HTTPS and goes directly from your device to the service. The Drive file sits in your own account, protected by your Google sign-in. We recommend turning on two-step verification.
  • Shared items, reactions and share links are end-to-end encrypted (AES-GCM, with keys exchanged using ECDH for shared items). The private half of your sharing key never leaves your device and your own Drive.
  • If you set a PIN, only a one-way hash of it is stored, and it never leaves your device. After five wrong tries Plotlines makes you wait before the next one, longer each time. The PIN keeps other people out of the app on a shared device. It does not encrypt your data.
  • Google access tokens are short-lived (about one hour) and are removed when you turn off sync or erase everything. Sign-ins are only accepted when Plotlines started them.
  • AI canvases run in a sandboxed frame without access to the app, and a canvas that uses your data can’t reach the network. Content from emails, web pages and shared links is shown as plain text, never run as code.

No method of storage or transmission is completely secure. We keep Plotlines’ design simple and local to reduce risk, but we cannot guarantee absolute security.

12. Keeping and deleting your data

Your data stays until you delete it. You can:

  • Delete single items (goals, steps, habits, moments, conversations, canvases, boards) inside the app. With sync on, deletions reach your other devices.
  • Erase everything on a device: Settings → Your data → Erase everything. This removes your plan, conversations, canvases, boards, Picture of me, settings, saved keys and sign-ins from that device. Downloaded AI models and voices stay, as they hold nothing of yours. Your Drive copy is kept so you can restore it.
  • Remove the app’s data completely: uninstall the Android app, or clear this site’s data in your browser settings for the web app.
  • Delete the synced copy in Google Drive: open drive.google.com → Settings (gear icon) → Manage apps → Plotlines → Options → Delete hidden app data.
  • Stop sharing an item or a link from the item itself; the shared copy is deleted.
  • Revoke Plotlines’ access to your Google account at myaccount.google.com/permissions, and to Notion in Notion’s integration settings.
  • Delete exported files (backups, calendar files, downloaded canvases, photos in Pictures/Plotlines) with your file manager or gallery.

13. Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR in Europe or the CCPA/CPRA in California), you may have the right to access, correct, delete, export or restrict the processing of your personal data. Plotlines gives you these controls directly: you can view and edit everything in the app, export a complete copy with Settings → Your data → Export backup, and delete everything as described in section 12.

Because we hold no copy of your data, we cannot view, change, export or delete it for you.

14. Children

Plotlines is intended for general audiences and is not directed at children under 13 (or the minimum age in your country). We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. Because Plotlines stores data only on your device and in your own accounts, we do not receive information from any user, including children. The assistant has a family-safe mode in Settings › AI & voice.

15. Changes and contact

If Plotlines’ data handling changes, for example a new feature that sends data somewhere new, we will update this page and its effective date before the change is released. Significant changes will also be noted in the app’s release notes. We will not start collecting data from you without telling you first.

Questions or requests about this policy or your privacy: open an issue at github.com/Munazzar/plotline/issues.

Plotlines

Privacy Policy

Effective October 21, 2026 · Applies to the Plotlines Android app and the Plotlines web app

The short version

  • Everything you write in Plotlines (goals, steps, habits, journal moments, photos) is stored on your own device.
  • Plotlines has no servers of its own and we never receive, see or store your data. The one exception you control: if you share a single goal, habit, step or list with someone, an end-to-end encrypted copy of just that item goes to Plotlines’ sharing space on Google Firebase (section 6).
  • Activity from your phone (steps, sleep, workouts, screen time) is read on the phone only when you allow it, and kept with the rest of your data.
  • AI features run on your device by default. Nothing goes to an AI service unless you set up your own AI server (section 5).
  • If you turn on sync, your data is copied to a private, hidden folder in your own Google Drive. Only Plotlines on your devices can read it, and only you can delete it.
  • No accounts with us, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, no cookies, and we never sell or share your data.
  • You can export or erase everything at any time.

1. Who we are

Plotlines is a personal planning app for life goals, daily goals, habits, routines and journaling. It is developed independently by Munazzar (“we”, “us”). This policy explains what information Plotlines handles, where it is kept, who can access it and how you control it.

Because Plotlines works without a server, we are not able to access your information. In data-protection terms, you are in control of your own data at all times.

2. What Plotlines handles

Content you create

  • Goals and sub-goals: titles, reasons (“why it matters”), life area, horizon, priority, dates, links between goals, cover images, pinned status and check-in times.
  • Steps: titles, notes, due dates and times, reminders, repeat settings, completion history and focus-timer minutes.
  • Daily goals (goals for a day) with dates, times and durations.
  • Habits and routines: names, schedules, amounts, reminders, routine steps, daily check-offs, rest days, pauses, and for habits you are breaking: start time, slips (time, trigger and optional note), urges resisted, daily counts and any cost or time figures you enter.
  • Journal moments: titles, text and photos, plus automatic entries such as completed steps and milestones.
  • Weekly reviews you write.
  • Threads (quick notes and chains of thought) and lists, with their items, due dates and check-offs.
  • Your own activity trackers (for example yoga minutes or pages read) and the amounts you log each day.
  • Activity read on your Android phone, only for the kinds you allow: steps (including steps by hour), distance, calories, sleep and workouts from Health Connect, your phone’s step counter, minutes spent in each app (screen time), and time spent at places you saved. It is kept for up to 180 days.
  • Insights Plot writes for you, and conversations with the assistant, if you use them.

Settings and preferences

Your first name (optional), theme and display choices, card styles, layout choices, reminder preferences, and a PIN if you set one (stored only as a one-way hash).

Google account details (only if you turn on sync)

The email address of the Google account you connect, and a short-lived access token that lets Plotlines read and write its own sync file. See section 4.

What Plotlines does not collect

Plotlines does not send us anything. It does not collect contacts, device identifiers, advertising IDs, usage analytics or crash reports. Activity, screen time and places (above) are read on your phone only after you allow them in Android, stay in your own data, and are never sent to us. See section 8 for address search.

4. Google sign-in and Drive sync

Sync is optional and off until you turn it on. When you connect Google Drive:

  • Sign-in. On Android, you pick a Google account on your device, or sign in through your browser. On the web, you sign in on Google’s own page. We never see your Google password.
  • Permission requested. Plotlines asks for one Google permission only: drive.appdata. It lets Plotlines create and read its own hidden file in your Drive. It does not let Plotlines see, open or change any of your other Drive files, and it gives no access to Gmail, Contacts, Photos or anything else.
  • Your email address. Plotlines reads the email address of the connected account, shows it in Settings, and uses it to confirm your identity if you reset a forgotten PIN. It is stored only on your device.
  • How data travels. Your device talks directly to Google’s servers over encrypted HTTPS connections. Nothing passes through any server of ours, because we don’t run one.
  • When it syncs. Sync runs when you make changes, when you open the app, every few minutes while the app is open, and when you pull down to refresh. If two devices changed the same item, the most recent change wins.
  • Turning it off. Settings → Sync → Turn off sync. That device stops syncing. Your Drive copy stays until you delete it (see section 11).

Plotlines’ use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Data from Google is used only to provide sync to you. It is never used for advertising, never sold, never transferred to others, and never read by a person.

5. AI and voice

Plotlines’ assistant (Plot), Insights, Plan with AI, the Agent and “Fill in today” for your trackers all use the AI you pick in Settings › AI & voice:

  • On-device AI (the default). An open-source model is downloaded once (from Hugging Face, with the code that runs it from jsDelivr) and then runs on your phone or in your browser. Your questions and data stay on your device.
  • Your own AI server, only if you enter one. The question you ask and the data needed to answer it are sent to the address you entered, and are handled under that server’s own terms. We never see them.
  • Copy and paste. Plan with AI can also build a prompt that you copy into an assistant of your choice (for example Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini) and paste the reply back. Read the prompt before you paste it, and leave out anything you don’t want to share.

What the AI is given: Insights get a short summary of your last two weeks (habit check-ins, goal progress and next steps, step, sleep and screen-time averages, tracker totals, and up to five recent journal entries). Fill in today gets today’s journal entries, thread notes, finished day goals and workouts. Entries you mark private are never included.

Voice. Talking to Plot and “Hey Plot” use speech recognition on your device (Android’s recognizer, or your browser’s on the web; some browsers send audio to their maker’s speech service, under that maker’s policy). Spoken replies use your device’s text-to-speech. Plotlines does not record or keep audio.

Agent connections. If you connect Google Calendar or Gmail in Settings › Agent & connections, the agent reads events or messages straight from Google when a task needs them, and the AI you picked works with what it reads. You can disconnect at any time.

6. Notifications, widgets, sharing and files

  • Reminders and notifications (step reminders, goal check-ins, habit reminders, focus timers, milestones) are scheduled on your device using Android’s alarm service, or by your browser while the web app is open. No push server is involved. Notification text can appear on your lock screen, depending on your device’s notification settings.
  • Home-screen widgets show titles, progress and habit status on your home screen. Anyone who can see your home screen can see them. Taps on widgets and on the “Mark done” button in notifications are stored on your device until the app next opens.
  • Sending a goal as text opens your device’s share sheet. Where it goes is up to you.
  • Doing it together. When you share one goal, habit, step or list with people you invite, an end-to-end encrypted copy of just that item (its title, steps and mode, and each person’s name, check-ins and reactions) is kept in Plotlines’ sharing space on Google Firebase so everyone in it stays in step. Only the people in it can read it. When the owner stops sharing, the shared copy is deleted; anyone who leaves has their progress removed from it.
  • Calendar export creates a standard calendar file that you open in your calendar app. That app then handles the events.
  • Links to AI assistants and to Google sign-in open in your browser or the relevant app.

7. Android permissions

Each one is asked for only when you use the feature that needs it, and you can turn any of them off in Android settings.

  • Internet: for sync, sharing, address search, downloading an on-device AI model, and your own AI server if you set one.
  • Notifications, exact alarms, full-screen alerts, run at startup, vibrate and wake lock: to deliver the reminders you set on time, restore them after a restart, and give feedback when you check something off.
  • Physical activity (activity recognition) and Health Connect read access to steps, distance, calories, sleep and exercise: for the Activity page and automatic habit check-offs. Plotlines never writes to Health Connect.
  • Usage access: to show minutes spent in each app (screen time). Only minutes per app are read, never what you do inside them.
  • Location, including in the background: only if you save a place for an automatic check-off (see below).
  • Microphone and a foreground service: for talking to Plot and “Hey Plot”, only while listening is on.
  • Biometrics: to unlock Plotlines with your fingerprint or face if you turn on App lock.
  • Display over other apps and ignore battery optimisation: so the assistant and reminders can appear when you call them, and are not stopped by battery saving.

Location is asked for only if you save a place for an automatic check-off, and is used on the phone to notice when you are there. Plotlines does not request camera, storage or contacts permissions. When you add a photo, Android’s own camera or file picker opens, and Plotlines receives only the image you choose.

8. Third-party services

  • Google (sign-in and Drive), only if you turn on sync. Google’s Privacy Policy applies to data stored in your Google account.
  • GitHub Pages hosts the web app. Like any web host, GitHub may record technical information such as your IP address when your browser loads the site. See GitHub’s Privacy Statement. The Android app loads its pages from inside the app. It contacts GitHub Pages only if you choose browser sign-in for sync, because Google returns you to the web app’s address to finish signing in.
  • Photon / OpenStreetMap (photon.komoot.io), only when you add a place: the words you type to search an address, and a rounded position (about 1 km) to rank results and suggest spots nearby. When you tap “use my location”, a position rounded to about 10 m is sent once to look up the address; moving the pin on the map does the same. The small map loads its images from OpenStreetMap’s tile server (tile.openstreetmap.org), which sees which area is shown. Nothing else is sent, and no account is involved.
  • Google Firebase, only when you share an item with others (section 6).
  • Hugging Face and jsDelivr, only to download an on-device AI model and the code that runs it. Like any download, they see your IP address.
  • Google Calendar and Gmail, only if you connect them to the agent (section 5).
  • AI assistants, AI servers and share targets you choose to use, as described in sections 5 and 6.

Plotlines includes no third-party SDKs, analytics libraries, advertising networks or crash reporters. Fonts are bundled with the app and are not loaded from outside services.

9. What we never do

  • Run servers or databases that hold your data.
  • Sell, rent or share your personal information.
  • Show ads or use your data for advertising.
  • Track you across apps or websites, or use cookies.
  • Collect analytics, usage statistics or crash reports.
  • Read your content, or train AI models on it.

10. Security

  • Your data lives inside Plotlines’ private storage on your device, which other apps cannot read. Modern Android and iOS devices encrypt this storage when the device is locked with a screen lock. We recommend using one.
  • Sync traffic travels only over encrypted HTTPS connections directly to Google. The Drive file sits in your own account, protected by your Google sign-in. We recommend turning on two-step verification.
  • If you set a PIN, only a one-way hash of it is stored, and it never leaves your device. The PIN keeps other people out of the app on a shared device. It does not encrypt your data.
  • Google access tokens are short-lived (about one hour) and are removed when you turn off sync.

No method of storage or transmission is completely secure. We keep Plotlines’ design simple and local to reduce risk, but we cannot guarantee absolute security.

11. Keeping and deleting your data

Your data stays until you delete it. You can:

  • Delete single items (goals, steps, habits, moments) inside the app. With sync on, deletions reach your other devices.
  • Erase everything on a device: Settings → Your data → Erase everything. This clears that device only. Your Drive copy is kept so you can restore it.
  • Remove the app’s data completely: uninstall the Android app, or clear this site’s data in your browser settings for the web app.
  • Delete the synced copy in Google Drive: open drive.google.com → Settings (gear icon) → Manage apps → Plotlines → Options → Delete hidden app data.
  • Revoke Plotlines’ access to your Google account at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Delete exported files (backups, calendar files, photos in Pictures/Plotlines) with your file manager or gallery.

12. Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR in Europe or the CCPA/CPRA in California), you may have the right to access, correct, delete, export or restrict the processing of your personal data. Plotlines gives you these controls directly: you can view and edit everything in the app, export a complete copy with Settings → Your data → Export backup, and delete everything as described in section 11.

Because we hold no copy of your data, we cannot view, change, export or delete it for you. If you have questions, contact us (section 15).

13. Children

Plotlines is intended for general audiences and is not directed at children under 13 (or the minimum age in your country). We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. Because Plotlines stores data only on your device and in your own Google account, we do not receive information from any user, including children.

14. Changes to this policy

If Plotlines’ data handling changes, for example a new feature that sends data somewhere new, we will update this page and its effective date before the change is released. Significant changes will also be noted in the app’s release notes. We will not start collecting data from you without telling you first.
